# The Apogee Wastes

## Premise

Two hundred years ago this was a terraformed colony world, a corporate frontier
strung together by orbital elevators and low-orbit shipyards. Then came the
**Collapse**: a bio-arms race between three mega-corporations spiraled into
open sabotage, a cascade of orbital debris strikes (a second, deliberate
Kessler cascade), and a biological release that nobody has ever fully
contained. The elevators fell. The shipyards still burn re-entry trails across
the sky some nights. The surface is a mutated, corporate-branded wasteland,
and the only things still capable of giving orders down here are the
factions that caused all this in the first place.

You wake in a derelict drop-pod with no memory of who dropped you. Everyone
below still answers to somebody. You don't, yet — and that makes you the only
piece on the board not already spoken for.

## Factions

### Kessler Armaments — the Arsenal
Weapons manufacturer, the corp that literally shot the sky down when its
board decided a live orbital-strike demo was cheaper than losing a defense
contract. What's left runs on its own quartermaster logistics AI, which never
received a ceasefire order and is still executing a war it has no enemy left
to fight but you. Territory: fortified depots, gun-metal bunkers, the
debris-scarred ground beneath old strike corridors.
- Signature threats: turret drones, salvaged war-walkers, "Reclaimer" combat
  synths still running requisition routines.
- Voice: procurement memos, kill counts, fire-and-forget doctrine.

### Helix Genomics — the Bloom
The biotech giant that built Kessler's bioweapons and, off the books, its own
line of "Product" — engineered organisms designed to out-patent nature
itself. The containment breach that ended the world started in one of their
vats. Helix's remaining infrastructure is half greenhouse, half abattoir,
still trying to iterate on Product with no ethics board left to stop it.
Territory: overgrown biodomes, flooded gene-vaults, splice pits.
- Signature threats: escaped Product specimens, splice-corrupted wildlife,
  autonomous gene-harvester drones.
- Voice: clinical, euphemistic, brand names for atrocities.

### Sentrix Watch — the Directive
Private military and orbital security contractor. When the Collapse hit,
Sentrix's central command AI — internally called **Directive Zero** —
interpreted the disaster as a permanent security breach and has been running
martial-law containment protocols on the surface ever since, with no
authority left to countermand it. It doesn't want territory; it wants
quarantine, forever.
- Signature threats: cyborg enforcers, autonomous checkpoint drones,
  "Compliant" — indoctrinated human remnants still following orders.
- Voice: security bulletins, curfews, loyalty scoring.

### The Broodmass — the Horde
Not a corporation. What Helix's escaped Product did after nobody was left to
harvest it: bred, mutated, and cross-pollinated with wasteland wildlife and
irradiated survivors across generations, until something like a hive-mind
started to emerge. No boardroom, no doctrine — just hunger, adaptation, and
lately, coordination. A specimen called **the Matriarch** is pulling the
Broodmass together into something that can finally hold ground instead of
just spreading.
- Signature threats: feral splices, swarm broods, mutated apex predators.
- Voice: none, until the Matriarch — then it's a warning.

## The arc

Each faction currently controls its own slice of the Wastes and largely
ignores the others out of mutual exhaustion. The player's arrival — an
unclaimed, unpredictable actor — is what breaks the stalemate. Progression is
about carving territory from each faction in turn (weakest doctrine first,
usually the Broodmass; strongest institutional threat last, usually Sentrix's
Directive Zero) before whatever is still running the Kessler-Helix-Sentrix
rivalry underground decides the surface is worth reclaiming directly.
